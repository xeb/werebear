Where the wolf has forever stood in folklore as the hungry devourer—the shadow on the edge of the winter sheepfold—the bear has always been something far more complicated: a brother in the dark, a king in exile, an earth-god clothed in hide and fat.

Across ancient Scandinavia, Anglo-Saxon Britain, and Native American medicine traditions, the man who becomes a bear does not lose his soul. He amplifies it. He trades the fragile bones of men for the unstoppable sinew of the forest warden. Laid out below are the ten greatest depictions ever committed to canvas, paper, cel, and film.

The Source Text · De Reconditis Hominum Formis · c. A.D. 1240

De Hominibus Qui
Fuerunt Ursi

Of Men Who Walked as Great Bears

“Non vt lupi rapaces per noctem grassantur, sed vt siluarum custodes, solo motu brachiorum quercus flectentes, dormientes in antris donec terra clamet.”

“They do not prowl through the night as ravenous wolves, but stand as wardens of the deep woods; bending oak trees by the mere movement of their arms, sleeping in caves until the earth itself cries out for their fury.”

From the recovered fragments of the Gesta Septentrionalia, recorded by Brother Aelfric of Northumbria. Unlike the wretched sufferers of lycanthropia, those blessed with the ursa-hamr were hailed as protectors of sacred boundaries, holy hermits, and kings who carried the bloodline of the mountain.

Marginalia · Six Essential Truths of the Werebear

Glosses on how the werebear survived the Middle Ages, reshaped modern fantasy literature, and became a beloved titan of pop culture.

I

The Root of 'Berserker'

The English word berserk derives directly from the Old Norse ber-sarkr: literally "bear-shirt." Elite Viking shock troops fought without chainmail, channeling the sacred frenzy of Odin by wrapping themselves in the spirit and pelts of bears.

II

Bǫðvarr Bjarki at Lejre

In Scandinavian lore, King Hrólfr Kraki's champion was Bǫðvarr Bjarki. As enemies breached the fortress, Bjarki slept in his tent while his enormous spirit bear killed more foes than five hundred warriors combined.

III

Tolkien's Beorn

In The Hobbit, J.R.R. Tolkien consciously restored the ancient Germanic archetype of the solitary, honey-eating vegetarian skin-changer who turns into an unstoppable Great Bear to crush the goblin host at the Battle of Five Armies.

IV

The D&D Moral Divergence

In Gary Gygax’s original Monster Manual (1977), while werewolves were Chaotic Evil and wereboars were Neutral, werebears were deliberately designated Lawful Good: benevolent hermits who heal wounded wanderers and slay evil predators.

V

The 2001 Card Pun

Carl Critchlow's Werebear in MTG's Odyssey set gave birth to the legendary flavor text: “He exercises his right to bear arms.” It remains one of the top five most famous card lines ever printed by Wizards of the Coast.

VI

The Mage Tower 'Hulk Bear'

In World of Warcraft: Legion, Blizzard unlocked the bipedal "Might of the Grizzlemaw" appearance for Guardian Druids. It replaced the four-legged bear with an ape-like, two-legged powerhouse with runic claws, instantly becoming the most coveted transmog in the game's 20-year history.
